Beat 'Em Up Games, Explained: Combos, Crowds & Bosses

A practical introduction to the side-scrolling action genre: what makes it distinct and how to think your way through a crowded fight.

By Mephitideus Interactive · Player guide

A beat 'em up is an action game built around fighting groups of opponents while moving through a stage. You control one character, read threats coming from several directions, and use movement and attacks together to make room. The fun is not only landing hits; it is deciding where to stand before the next group closes in.

Beat 'em up or fighting game?

The names sound similar, but the usual structure is different. A traditional fighting game focuses on a one-on-one match in a contained arena, with each player reading a single opponent. A beat 'em up usually sends one player against multiple enemies across a sequence of spaces. Some games scroll from left to right; others use arenas or platforming routes. The shared thread is close-range action against groups.

That changes the player's question. Instead of asking only, “How do I beat this opponent's move?”, you also ask, “Which threat needs attention first, and where will I be safe after this attack?”

Four habits that make crowded fights easier

  1. Keep moving with a purpose. Standing still lets enemies approach from both sides. Move to create space, draw opponents into a smaller area, or reach a platform that changes the angle of the fight.
  2. Prioritize the threat, not just the nearest target. A ranged enemy can keep interrupting you while a slower opponent occupies the foreground. Scan the group before committing to a long attack sequence.
  3. Use attacks to change position as well as deal damage. A dash, launcher, or area attack can interrupt a cluster or help cross a dangerous gap. Save a move with a longer recovery for a moment when fewer enemies can punish it.
  4. Protect the rhythm of a combo. In games that reward consecutive defeats, the clock between hits matters. Take a short route to the next enemy rather than extending a combo with a risky attack that leaves you surrounded.

Boss fights ask for a different rhythm

A boss is usually less about clearing a crowd and more about recognizing a repeated pattern. Watch for a wind-up, move out of the attack lane, and use the recovery moment to respond. If the boss changes behavior as its health falls, treat each change as new information rather than repeating the same approach faster.

These habits transfer between games, but the exact timing and move set do not. Read the controls, try a safe attack, and learn how much time each move leaves you before choosing a longer combo.
